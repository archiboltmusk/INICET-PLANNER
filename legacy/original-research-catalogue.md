# INI-CET integrated study catalogue

Research cut-off: 27 September 2026. Window: 27 September 2016–27 September 2026.

Completed deliverable: 19 integrated blocks, 57 study targets, 120 selected recall records, and historical counts for all 19 subjects. This is a curated evidence-based catalogue, **not a complete census of ten years of papers**. All exam-topic records are publisher/educator recall claims; none are official authenticated question papers. Missing entries do not mean a topic was absent.

## What the evidence supports

1. Revisit concepts in different forms. For example, superior-gluteal supply is reported in May 2022 and May 2023; PPH drug selection appears in May 2024 and May 2025; optic neuritis appears in May 2022 and May 2026. Dates and locators are in the ledger. These are concept recurrences, not proven verbatim repeats.

2. Integration is longstanding: the 2017 publisher sample links ECG and heart sounds, while 2026 recalls link ECG to coronary anatomy. It is not defensible to claim that integration suddenly started in a particular recent year.

3. Old AIIMS-PG papers are useful for mechanisms and images. Their subject proportions cannot be pooled with modern INI-CET as a fixed blueprint. AIIMS records the transition in 2020 [A20].

4. Recent reports disagree about subject dominance. November 2024 student feedback highlights ENT/eye/anatomy but also makes inconsistent broader claims [C24]. Do not omit smaller subjects on that basis.

5. Current treatments, legal rules and screening programmes require current references. A recalled question documents a tested task, not a reliable answer key.

## How to read the catalogue

H = first-pass priority; M = second-pass priority. Both are study judgments. Difficulty 1 = mainly recognition, 2 = interpretation/comparison, 3 = multi-step reasoning. These are estimated task demands, not measured candidate difficulty. Recurrence ranks: concept recurrence, family recurrence, single/undated evidence, extension only. Counts reflect only the selected ledger and are lower bounds on reports found, not frequency estimates.

The practice variations are original preparation suggestions. They are **not predicted exam questions**. No probability or guarantee is assigned.

BTR A = file ending 1-600.pdf; B = file ending 601-1015.pdf. Locators are 1-based PDF pages within that file (B page 1 corresponds to combined page 601). Only A:378 was visually inspected in the pilot; other pointers are verified text hits and may miss handwriting. Harrison chapter numbers were checked against the supplied contents; they are reading pointers, not claims of full-chapter medical validation. Use BTR for revision; Harrison selectively for mechanisms and confusing medicine differentials. Surgical, OBG, eye and ENT detail needs its own subject resource.

## Corrections and source exclusions

- BTR A:378 labels DPP-4 drugs as contraindicated in renal failure except linagliptin. Do not learn that as a class rule: sitagliptin prescribing information provides renal dose adjustment, including severe impairment/ESRD [F1].

- The same BTR table groups tirzepatide with GLP-1 drugs. Retain its more precise dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonism [F2].

- PrepLadder duplicates May 2025 questions across subjects. Reflex explicitly adds some high-yield practice questions. Duplicates and labelled additions were not treated as separate PYQs.

- Several recall keys contain inconsistencies. For example, the May 2026 DigiNerve renal stem states raised C3 alongside a post-streptococcal pattern; its amenorrhoea and spinal-TB keys also require independent review. These disputed items were excluded from the evidence ledger. Other included entries record tasks only, without endorsing their answers.

- The generic DigiNerve 2025 page does not clearly label the sitting and includes a later-year Nobel item; it was excluded from dated counts.

- DocTutorials percentages were rejected: the November 2025 page reports only 20–25 preclinical questions yet also claims anatomy alone is 43% of the paper. Those numbers cannot both describe 200 questions. General expected-weightage tables without a reproducible denominator were also excluded.
